Species Distribution Modelling: Podocarpus latifolius

Project Overview

This project investigates the distribution of Podocarpus latifolius (Real Yellowwood) in South Africa using Species Distribution Modelling (SDM). It examines the relationship between species occurrence and environmental conditions across past, current and future climate scenarios.

Objectives

* Model the potential distribution of Podocarpus latifolius under different climatic conditions.
* Identify the environmental variables contributing most to predicted species distributions.
* Compare distribution patterns across past, current and future climate scenarios.
* Evaluate model performance using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves and Area Under the Curve (AUC).
* Interpret model predictions in relation to species biology, ecology and climate change.
* Assess limitations associated with occurrence data, environmental variables and species distribution models.

Methodology

The analysis used MaxEnt (Maximum Entropy) to model species distributions using species occurrence records and environmental data.

Part 1: Scenario-Specific Models

Separate models were developed using species occurrence data and environmental variables for:

* Current climatic conditions
* Future climatic conditions
* Holocene climatic conditions

Part 2: Climate Projections

The model developed under current climatic conditions was projected onto past and future environmental conditions. These projections were compared with the independently developed models from Part 1.

Analysis and Outputs

The project includes:

* Species distribution maps showing predicted suitability and occurrence locations.
* ROC curves and AUC measures for model performance.
* Environmental response curves illustrating relationships between predictor variables and predicted occurrence.
* Tables showing the percentage contributions of environmental predictor variables.
* Comparisons of past, current and future distribution patterns.
* Ecological interpretations of predicted distributions and potential climate change impacts.

Key Areas of Investigation

The analysis examines the importance of temperature and precipitation variables in predicting species distribution, changes in predicted suitability across climate scenarios, and the relationship between model predictions and the species’ ecological requirements.

It also considers potential sampling bias in species occurrence records, spatial and temporal mismatches between occurrence and environmental data, and limitations of climate-based distribution predictions.

Tools and Data

* MaxEnt: Species distribution modelling.
* GBIF: Source of species occurrence records.
* Environmental climate data: Past, current and future climate scenarios.
* Model outputs: Distribution maps, ROC/AUC graphs, response curves and predictor contribution tables.
